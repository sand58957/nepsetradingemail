Opt-in collection, step by step

  1. Conversational commerce. Settle what this has to produce before touching any configuration — the required output is what dictates the setup.
  2. Broadcast strategy. Write down who owns this and how often it gets reviewed. Unowned steps are where campaigns quietly break.
  3. Session windows. Get this working for a single segment first. A narrow test surfaces problems that a full rollout only hides.
  4. Catalogues. Record the state you are starting from, so the change you make next can actually be attributed.
  5. Review. Re-read the result against the original goal for opt-in collection and cut whatever does not serve it.

Where this usually goes wrong

  • Treating catalogues as a one-off. It drifts, and the drift stays invisible until performance moves.
  • Skipping business API vs Business App because it is not urgent. The cost shows up later as variance nobody can explain.
  • Copying someone else's campaign types wholesale. The settings that suit their audience rarely suit yours.
  • Changing opt-in rules and something else in the same week. You lose the ability to attribute the result.

How to tell it worked

Published benchmarks for opt-in collection vary so widely by audience and sector that borrowing one tends to mislead. Your own account is a better reference, and you already hold the data.

  1. Pick a single metric that reflects opt-in collection rather than general activity.
  2. Take a baseline over a period long enough to cover your normal sending cycle.
  3. Change one variable, hold the rest steady, and let the test run to a stable sample.
  4. Compare against your own baseline, not an industry figure of unknown provenance.

Numbers produced this way describe your audience specifically, which is the only comparison that reliably informs a decision.
